# Vidhara — Decision Log

> **Status**: Living document — append-only. · **Last updated**: 2026-08-16 (D-065)
> Product/strategy decisions with rationale and a revisit trigger. Architecture decisions stay in `architecture.md` §16 (ADR-1…11); this log covers product strategy from the 2026-07-16 lean reset onward. Updated the day a decision is made — not at release boundaries.

**D-001 · 2026-07-16 · Adopt release-based lean roadmap; phases.md superseded for forward planning.**
Context: Phase 1–8 plan deferred user contact by months and pre-committed features on zero evidence; founder called it out explicitly. Decision: releases V0.1→V2.0 with pre-registered gates ([release-plan.md](release-plan.md)); phases.md retained as historical build log only. Revisit: never (direction), quarterly (calendar).

**D-002 · 2026-07-16 · One wedge, one customer: old⇄new law mapping for law students & judiciary aspirants.**
Context: the vision spans ≥4 distinct products/customers. The 2023→new-codes transition is an urgent, universal, *expiring* pain in the student/aspirant segment, and our 1,271-mapping corpus is the only clean asset addressing it. Decision: everything not serving this wedge is FUTURE or REMOVE ([feature-priority.md](feature-priority.md)). Advocates are an expansion segment, not a launch segment. Revisit: G1 pivot review if A-1 fails; also if organic *advocate* usage appears unprompted.

**D-003 · 2026-07-16 · Web-first for validation; Android runs in parallel, launches at V1.0.**
Context: ADR-8 says Android-first — correct for the long-term product (audience is mobile-first), but Play's 12-tester × 14-day closed-test requirement gates a personal account's production access, while the web (already SEO-structured) deploys in days and shares cleanly into Telegram/WhatsApp. Decision: learning happens on web from V0.1; the Play clock starts at V0.2 so Android production lands inside V1.0. This amends ADR-8's *go-to-market order*, not the architecture (Expo app remains the flagship surface). Revisit: if web traction is blocked by mobile-web friction in beta interviews.

**D-004 · 2026-07-16 · No AI before Gate G1; first AI is a scoped, eval-gated, section-grounded explainer.**
Context: AI Tutor is the vision centerpiece, but wrong legal answers to exam-takers are trust-fatal, and a general tutor has unbounded scope/cost. Decision: fake-door demand test first (V0.5); if chosen at G1, ship only behind the E-7 eval bar (~0 factual errors on a 50-pair hand-checked set), answers always citing + displaying the underlying section; kill-switch feature flag; Sev-0 on any user-reported factual error. Revisit: at G1 with data.

**D-005 · 2026-07-16 · Kill list moved to parking lot with unlock conditions.**
Simulator, Moot Court, AI Judge, Virtual Internship, Advocate Workspace, Drafting, Community, Leaderboards, Achievements, Calendar, user-facing Analytics — each is a different startup or a post-PMF luxury ([future-ideas.md](future-ideas.md) has per-item reopen conditions). Revisit: only via those conditions.

**D-006 · 2026-07-16 · No monetization before Gate G2.**
Charging before retention measures churn speed, not willingness-to-pay. Free tier permanently includes the full corpus + mapping (acquisition engine, public good, SEO moat); the paid layer will be whatever V1.1/V1.2 *proves*. Old pricing guess (₹149/₹399) retired until E-8 pricing interviews. Revisit: G2 (2026-11-03).

**D-007 · 2026-07-16 · No account requirement to read; bookmarks/recents are local-first.**
A signup wall in front of an unproven product measures friction tolerance, not demand, and suppresses the retention signal we exist to measure. Accounts arrive when they carry user value (sync) or business necessity (payments, V2.0). Revisit: if local-bookmark loss (device switch/clear) surfaces as a top-3 interview complaint.

**D-008 · 2026-07-16 · Analytics stack: PostHog (cookieless, anonymous) + Sentry + UptimeRobot; event schema versioned in analytics-plan.md.**
Free tiers, zero infra, sufficient through V2.0. Instrumentation breakage is treated as an outage (a blind week = a lost week of learning). Raw lookup/search text never enters analytics (privacy). Revisit: at event-volume or team growth pressure.

**D-009 · 2026-07-16 · Beta design: 30–50 recruited users, 3-week window, D7 ≥25% bar, feature freeze during the window.**
Small enough to talk to everyone, large enough for cohort math to mean something; warm-cohort D7 below 25% ≈ absent pull. Freeze because shipping mid-window confounds the cohort. Revisit: n/a — one-shot design, judged at the V0.5 gate.

**D-010 · 2026-07-16 · Fake doors permitted, with honest copy only.**
"Coming soon — tap to tell us you want this" (no fake functionality, no bait UI that pretends to work). Measures curiosity cheaply; always cross-checked against interviews before money is spent (E-5). Revisit: if users express annoyance in feedback (then stop immediately — trust > signal).

**D-011 · 2026-07-16 · Content policy: source-faithful text, bundles as artifacts of record, corrections are Sev-0.**
The corpus is the moat and the trust anchor. India Code's own body-heading typos are preserved (documented in phases.md 2026-07-16 entries) pending an editorial-emendation policy; any user-reported *parsing* error outranks feature work. New acts are added on demand-evidence, not completionism (pipeline makes each act ~days). Revisit: emendation policy decision when search-miss data or user reports force it.

**D-012 · 2026-07-16 · Documentation cadence: decision-log same-day; release docs at release boundaries; docs never block a ship.**
Ten strategy docs + six build docs is a lot for a solo founder; the failure mode is documentation-as-procrastination. The only artifact that matters in any given week is the shipped release and the Monday numbers. Revisit: if docs drift is causing real mistakes (then simplify the doc set, don't write more).

**D-015 · 2026-07-17 · Chapter titles repaired corpus-wide (text rule + curated set); suggestions channel opened.**

This is a narrow, verifiable exception to "no content from model memory" (D-011/ADR-6): chapter *names* are famous, low-risk, and cross-checked against ingested data; mappings/statute text remain strictly source-only. Known theoretical edge of the text rule ("A NEW …") documented in code; audit new acts' chapter diffs on ingest. Also: migration 0006 adds `feedback.kind` ('rating'|'suggestion', score nullable with shape check) powering the /feedback page (web) + Profile suggestion form (Android) — the founder-requested "tell us what to improve" channel. Revisit: if a future re-ingest with PDFs disagrees with any curated title, the parser wins.

**D-017 · 2026-07-19 · Content-trust proofread: stripped 176 leaked headings from section bodies (live DB + bundles); dropped-illustrations bug logged for re-ingest.**
Context: spot-check of the most-cited sections (starting with the reported IPC §420 leak) exposed a *systematic* parser bug — group/part/chapter headings that belong to the *next* section leaked onto the END of the prior section's body ("…liable to fine. **Of Fraudulent Deeds and Dispositions of Property**"). A conservative sweep found **176** instances across all 8 acts: "Of …" group headings (IPC/BNS), lettered sub-parts "B.—…" (CrPC/BNSS), bracketed "[CHAPTER …]/[PART …]" markers, and garbled "C II.—…" chapter abbreviations (COI/IEA). Decision: strip them surgically — regex removes only the trailing heading, **keeping** the sentence terminator and any "Illustration(s)" marker (so genuine missing-content stays visible). Applied to `scripts/ingest/bundles/*.json` (artifacts of record) AND the live DB via idempotent, id-preserving, `version`-bumping `UPDATE`s (residual scan → 0). All 40 ingest tests green; bundles re-validate. **Second bug logged, NOT fixed here (needs re-ingest):** the inline/bbox parser's small-font footnote filter also dropped **illustration text** — **153 sections** (IPC 64, ICA 88, CrPC 1) now end at a bare "Illustration(s)" heading with the examples gone (e.g. IPC §300 murder, §378 theft). No source PDFs are committed, so this requires re-download + an illustration-aware parser pass. Gotcha re-confirmed: bundle slug `coi` ≠ DB slug `constitution` (the 52 COI updates no-op'd until re-run with the DB slug — see D-016). Note: section pages are ISR-cached 1h with no on-demand revalidate route, so a content fix shows on fresh renders immediately but cached pages lag ≤1h. Revisit: schedule the illustration re-ingest before V0.5 cohort; consider an on-demand `revalidatePath` route so content fixes go live instantly.

**D-018 · 2026-07-19 · Illustrations restored (178 sections, +~127 KB of statute text) via illustration-aware re-ingest of IPC/ICA/CrPC; parity-checked, zero regressions.**
Context: D-017's proofread found the deeper bug — `gazette-inline.ts` dropped every word under 8.6pt to kill footnotes, but ILLUSTRATIONS are set in the same ~8.1–8.2pt type, so the worked examples vanished from 153+ sections (IPC §300 murder, §378 theft, §415 cheating, ICA §23…). Height cannot separate the two; **position and shape can**: illustrations follow an "Illustration(s)" heading and end at the next body-height line; footnotes sit at page bottom and open "N. Subs./Ins. … by Act …". Decision: parser keeps small-type lines only inside an illustration block (heading→body-line state machine; survives page breaks — 25 blocks wrap pages; page-scoped footnote latch verified against all 182 footnote blocks incl. 11 direct collisions; letterless small lines = superscript refs, always dropped; `keepIllustrations:false` escape hatch replicates legacy byte-for-byte). Sources re-acquired identically (ICA/CrPC byte-identical from indiacode via dns.google→Akamai; IPC from the exact Wayback snapshot 20200421003546); **parity check: keep=false output == pre-D-017 bundles across all 1,386 sections — 0 mismatches**, so the merge could safely take the new bodies (D-015/D-017 curation re-applied post-parse and preserved). Result: 178 sections gain their illustrations (IPC 74, ICA 97, CrPC 7; 1,243 lines, ~127 KB); IEA unaffected (its PDF sets illustrations at body height). Published via the repo's edge-function pattern (v16 one-time-token delta publisher → 178/178 rows, version+1 → tombstoned v17); provenance updated on all 3 acts (bundle + DB). Corpus-wide scan: **0 sections end at a bare "Illustration(s)" marker**. 6 new parser unit tests (46 total). Note: ICA prints a few footnotes at full body height — those were always kept (pre-existing, source-visible; parity-covered), a proofread item for later, not a regression. Revisit: run the D-017/D-018 tail-scan + bare-marker scan as standing checks in the V1.0 random-50 content QA.

**D-019 · 2026-07-24 · First AI feature shipped — "Explain this section", grounded + cache-first + provider-swappable; Gemini (free) not Anthropic; dark until the key lands.**
Context: D-004 gated AI to a scoped, eval-gated, section-grounded explainer; the founder chose a **free** provider (Gemini `gemini-2.0-flash`) over Anthropic to avoid per-token cost at scale, and approved building it now. Grounding is the whole game (wrong legal answers are trust-fatal, D-011): the Edge Function `explain-section` fetches the section's own published text + its old↔new mapping **server-side** and passes ONLY that to the model — the client sends `{slug, number}` and cannot inject text; the SYSTEM prompt forbids outside law, advice, and invented cross-references; the statute text stays on-screen beside the explanation with a "verify against the official text · not legal advice" disclaimer. Decision: (a) **cache-first** makes the free tier viable at scale — a section's text never changes, so it's explained once (first viewer) into `ai_explanations` and served to everyone after; cache hits cost nothing and consume no quota. (b) A global per-day counter `ai_usage` (`AI_DAILY_CAP`, default 800) soft-caps generation to protect the free quota; over cap → friendly 429. (c) Provider isolated in `callLLM()` (ADR-5) for a one-function swap. (d) `GEMINI_API_KEY` is a **Supabase Edge Function secret** — never in git/clients (rules §11); until it's set the function returns 503 and the UI shows "AI explanations are being set up." — so the feature ships dark and **activates the moment the founder adds the key**, no redeploy. Migration 0009 adds both tables, RLS-on with **no anon policies** (service-role only). Reader UI replaces the `ai_explain` fake door on **both** web + mobile with the real button + `ai_explain_requested` analytics event; a `pnpm --filter @nexlex/ingest ai-eval` harness samples real published sections across all 8 acts, prints source-beside-explanation for human grounding review, and red-flags likely-invented cross-references — the accuracy gate before the feature is trusted. All three packages typecheck clean; web round-trip verified live (click → event → live function → 503 → friendly message rendered). Revisit: **run `ai-eval` and eyeball the output the moment the key is set** — do not trust it until the eval passes; tune `AI_DAILY_CAP` off real usage; the fake-door demand signal on `ai_explain` ends here (this replaces it).

**D-022 · 2026-07-24 · Daily MCQ shipped early (V1.1 candidate B) — corpus-grounded, deterministic, replaces the fake door.**

**D-023 · 2026-07-24 · "Ask → routed to the real law" (grounded AI librarian); explicitly NOT an answer bot — D-004 line held.**
Context: founder wanted to expand the AI beyond section-explaining — "ask different things", surface it on home, and (their words) *"instead of just giving the answer it redirects the user there / gives them the article to read."* Decomposed into (a) grounded routing/retrieval vs (b) open-ended legal Q&A. (b) is the exact *"biggest trap"* D-004 names (wrong legal answers are trust-fatal) and is gated behind proven retention in feature-priority.md — declined for now. Founder chose (a). Decision: an `ask` Edge Function whose model has ONE job — translate a layperson's phrasing into formal **statutory search phrases** (e.g. "anticipatory bail" → "bail to person apprehending arrest"). Those phrases run against the REAL corpus (`search_sections` RPC), so **every result is an actual, linkable section** — the AI is a librarian, never an oracle: it never answers the legal question, never asserts a section number, and degrades to plain FTS if the model is down. Wiring: web `/search` runs plain FTS **first** (fast, no AI/no cost) and only calls `ask` when FTS returns **zero** (the NL/colloquial-term case), then renders the real sections with an *"interpreted as '<phrase>'"* transparency line + "read the section itself to confirm". Home `LandingLookup` + `SearchBox` copy now invite questions ("anticipatory bail", "punishment for cheating"); exact refs still route directly (`parseSectionRef`, no AI). Mobile Library search has the same FTS→`ask` fallback + note (reuses HitRow). Analytics `ask_ai_assisted {found}` both platforms; page reframed "Ask or search". Verified live: "anticipatory bail" → CrPC §438 / BNSS §482 (which plain FTS misses entirely); "cheating" stays on the fast FTS path (no AI). Honest limit: retrieval is grounded, not omniscient — a weak match surfaces a visibly-off real heading, **never a false legal claim** (that's the safety win vs an answer bot). Revisit: if retrieval quality becomes the bottleneck, add pgvector semantic search; the open-ended answer bot stays gated behind retention (D-004) — build it on data, not enthusiasm.

**D-025 · 2026-07-25 · Content-QA scanner + 85 repealed-section repairs; ICA §2/§3 boundary failure logged, NOT guessed.**
Context: funding-gap fortnight, item 🅑 — content trust is the moat and the clause-level proofread was still pending. Pulling all 29 in full showed the label "29 SEV2" was hiding three unrelated problems, and only one of them was fixable today. **(A) Ten bodies carried appended non-statute text** — cross-headings, footnote apparatus and, in one case, an entire Schedule. **(B) Three bodies are genuinely TRUNCATED** — TP §126 stops at "the right to take back at", ISA §281 at "declare that", NI §52 mid-illustration at a comma. **(C) Six are correct but for a missing full stop**, in illustrations the print itself mangles, exactly as D-063 described. Only (A) was touched, and the reason is the rule that has governed this corpus since D-011: **the missing words in (B) would have to come from the source, and there is no source PDF on disk.** Writing them from recall is precisely what D-031 records going wrong on NDPS §5. **Why the trim rule missed these in the first place, measured rather than guessed.** D-063's flush-time rule is `/^([\s\S]*[.;:?!”’")\]])\s+(\S[^.;:?!]{0,60})$/` plus guards, and three separate properties of it let these through: POCSO §4 and §10 end in the heading markers "B.—A" and "E.—S", which **contain a period**, and the tail capture excludes `.` — so the guard written for that very shape ("a heading marker like B.—A carries a period without being prose") never got to run, because the capture failed first. CPC §67's trailing cross-heading is **84 characters**, over the rule's 60. CRPC §484 carries several stacked markers at once. **The parser was deliberately NOT changed.** No source PDFs are on disk, so the regression D-063 insists on — old parser versus new across every act — cannot be run, and D-056 is the written record of what widening these rules unmeasured costs (216 difference groups, then 22, ICA §§89-90 and TP §80 dropped, a phantom MV §2B). A fix that cannot be measured is not ready, whatever it looks like on the three cases in front of you. **The repair is a cut, never a write.** `scripts/ingest/src/repairs/repair-category-a.mjs` names, per section, the exact string where the non-statute text begins, keeps everything before it and drops the rest. Each anchor must appear **exactly once** — zero means already repaired (so re-running is safe) and more than one means the anchor is ambiguous; both refuse. Two further guards refuse a cut that would remove more than 70% of a body or leave a remainder not ending like a sentence, which is what a wrong boundary looks like. **The largest find was not in the scanner's sample: CRPC §484 "Repeal and savings" had absorbed the entire First Schedule** — 1,747 characters of "THE FIRST SCHEDULE / CLASSIFICATION OF OFFENCES" and its chapter listing, displayed to any reader as part of the repeal provision. That is the schedule trap of D-031/D-033 surviving in an act ingested before those guards existed. CPC §67's first cut was also too shallow — trimming only the drop-cap-split cross-heading ("D elegation to C ollector of P ower") left the whole footnote block behind, so the anchor moved back to where the section actually ends, at sub-section (3). **Applied bundle-first**, per D-033: the ten bundles were repaired and the seven acts republished through `ingest publish`, so bundle and database cannot drift the way D-033 found them drifting. GCA was published first alone and verified before the acts carrying mappings were touched. **Verified:** 1,271 mappings intact and CrPC §154→BNSS 173, §438→BNSS 482, §125→BNSS 144 all still resolve (the republish preserves section ids, which is what mappings hang off); 5,594 sections, 36 acts; `body_plain` recomputed from `body_md` rather than copied stale (D-027's trap); content-qa **0 SEV1, SEV2 29 → 19**; the bundle diff is exactly ten changed lines across seven files. **Revisit:** category (B) needs the source PDFs re-fetched — three sections are missing statute text right now and no amount of parser work substitutes for the document; the parser fix for the trim rule needs those same sources before it can be regression-tested; and TP §121's remaining "warr ants" drop-cap artifact is a different defect class that this pass did not address.

**D-067 · 2026-08-16 · Illustrations whose continuation is set at footnote height — 64 lines of statute that no check could see. SEV2 19 → 14.**
Context: D-066 left three sections cut mid-phrase (TP §126 at "the right to take back at", ISA §281, NI §52) and said they needed the source. Re-fetching turned a three-section repair into a parser defect with corpus-wide reach. **Every source re-fetched verified against its own provenance:** TP, ISA, NI, CPC, CRPC and ICA came back **byte-identical** (565,764 / 694,325 / 681,124 / 3,275,360 / 1,879,339 / 568,075 bytes, ICA's sha256 matching D-027's record); IPC has no byte count recorded, so it was verified functionally — the Wayback snapshot D-018 names (20200421003546, found again through the CDX index) parses to **574 sections, exactly the committed set**. That verification is the whole basis for trusting anything below: a diff against a re-issued edition would prove nothing. **The defect.** D-018 established that illustrations are set in the same ~8.1-8.2pt type as footnotes and separated the two by position and shape — a block opens on an "Illustration(s)" heading and closes "at the next body-height line". That was measured, and it is true of the acts it was measured against. It is **false** for Transfer of Property, Negotiable Instruments, Civil Procedure and Criminal Procedure, which print an illustration's **first line at body height (9.96pt) and only its wrapped continuation at 8.10pt** — Negotiable Instruments §52 switches height *mid-line*, "…B indorses it to C," at 9.96 and "who indorses it to A." at 8.10. So the block closed on the illustration's own opening line, and every continuation was then read as a footnote and dropped. **Why nothing caught it for a month: a continuation dropped after a full stop leaves a body that still ends like a sentence.** Only the three cases where the cut happened to land mid-phrase ever reached the scanner. Measured against the sources, **64 illustration lines were absent across four acts** (CPC 38, NI 13, CRPC 8, TP 5) — and the first measurement of that overstated it, because matching on the letter marker "(a )" produced false positives where the parser had simply dropped the marker and kept the text; the figure above is after tightening the probe to an interior phrase. **The fix is two lines.** A body-height line no longer closes a block; only the heading opens one, and `flush` closes it — an illustration block belongs to the section it illustrates, so it ends where that section does. Footnotes are unaffected because they are excluded earlier, by the page-scoped latch and the FOOTNOTE_START shape, both of which run before the illustration branch. **The first attempt was wrong and the regression caught it.** Holding blocks open to the end of a section let the Indian Succession Act's per-page collector watermark — the third-party stamp D-062 recorded on that PDF — into **34 bodies**. The small-type path had never consulted `FURNITURE` at all, because while blocks closed at the first body-height line no furniture survived inside one long enough to matter. Adding that check, and the watermark to `FURNITURE`, cleared it; measured zero matches for that pattern in the other six acts. **Regression: old parser vs new across all seven acts on disk, 2,240 sections.** Section sets identical everywhere, **0 marginal notes changed, 0 bodies lost statute text**. Eleven bodies gained illustration text and two lost exactly the watermark string and nothing else — every one inspected individually, not sampled: IPC §330 (the zamindar illustration) and §499, ICA §29 and §153, TP §126, NI §4/§40/§50/§51/§52, CRPC §215 (+1,674 chars of the defective-charge illustrations). **Merged surgically, not wholesale** (D-037/D-054): eleven sections whose bundle matched the old parse took the new body; **IPC §330 and NI §4 carry curated repairs**, so only the pure-suffix delta was appended and the curation left intact — the script refuses any curated section whose change is not a pure suffix. Three new tests pin the behaviour, including that footnotes following an illustration block are still dropped, which is the whole risk of the change. **Verified:** 119 ingest tests; 1,271 mappings, 5,594 sections, 36 acts unchanged after six republishes; content-qa **0 SEV1, SEV2 19 → 14**. **Revisit:** CPC's 38 missing lines did **not** come back — its illustrations fail for a different reason this fix does not reach, and that is the next thing to measure; ISA §281 is still truncated, since that act needs D-062's `--rule-delimited` mode which was not run here; and the source PDFs are now on disk under `scripts/ingest/.sources/` (gitignored, reproducible from the provenance in each bundle), so the next parser change can be regressed without re-fetching.
